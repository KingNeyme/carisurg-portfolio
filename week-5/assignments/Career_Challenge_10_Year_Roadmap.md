# Career Challenge: 10-Year Roadmap

## Ideal Role 10 Years From Now

Ten years from now, I want to be working as an AI engineer and healthcare technology builder who can move between technical implementation, clinical problem-solving, and product strategy. My ideal role would sit at the intersection of applied machine learning, backend systems, and responsible clinical AI. I want to be someone who can build useful systems, explain them clearly to non-technical teams, and make sure the work is safe enough for real healthcare environments.

In a strong version of that future, I am either leading AI engineering work in a health-tech company or building my own Caribbean-focused healthcare technology product. The work would not just be about creating models. It would include data pipelines, evaluation, governance, user research, deployment, monitoring, and collaboration with clinicians.

## Working Backwards

| Timeframe | Milestones |

| Years 8-10 | Become a senior AI engineer, technical lead, founder, or applied AI specialist focused on healthcare systems that are reliable, explainable, and useful in Caribbean clinical contexts. |

## Structured Path vs. Unstructured Path

A structured path would look like: finish BSc, complete a Master’s, get a junior role, move into mid-level AI/data engineering, then grow into senior technical leadership. The benefit is clarity. It gives me credentials, a recognised progression, and access to academic research networks. The downside is that it can become too rigid if I only chase qualifications without building real systems.

An unstructured path would look more like: build projects, learn through work, join startups, freelance, contribute to open-source, and maybe create a product. The benefit is speed and practical experience. It could help me learn by solving real problems earlier. The downside is that it requires more self-direction, and I would need to be careful not to skip fundamentals.

The best path for me is probably a hybrid. I want enough structure to build depth, but enough flexibility to take opportunities when they appear. If a Master’s degree supports the work I want to do, I should pursue it. If a strong job, research role, or startup opportunity gives me better growth at that time, I should not ignore it just because it is not the traditional route.

## Flexible 10-Year Trajectory

My first priority is to become technically reliable. That means writing clean Python, understanding data deeply before modelling, using Git properly, building APIs, and learning how models are deployed and monitored. The CariSurg portfolio is part of that because it helps me practise healthcare-specific thinking: data quality, clinical workflows, ethics, safety, and communication.

After that, I want to build real experience. I would like to work in roles where I can combine business analysis, backend systems, data engineering, and AI. I already see value in being someone who understands both the business side and the technical side. In healthcare, that combination matters because the best model is not always the best solution if it does not fit the workflow.

Longer term, I want to specialise in clinical AI and responsible healthcare technology. That could lead to a Master’s or even a PhD, but I would only choose that if it supports a clear direction. I am also interested in entrepreneurship, especially if I find a healthcare problem in the Caribbean that technology can genuinely help solve.

My flexible plan is to keep building evidence of skill every year: better projects, stronger code, clearer writing, and more real-world impact. The exact job title may change, but the direction is clear: I want to become an AI engineer who can build systems that are technically strong, clinically aware, and useful for the communities they are meant to serve.
