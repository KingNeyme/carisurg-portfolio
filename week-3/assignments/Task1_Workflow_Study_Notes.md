# Task 1: ED Triage Workflow Study Notes

## Purpose

These are structured first-pass notes for mapping the Mercer General emergency department triage workflow.

**Validation requirement:** These notes are based on the Week 3 scenario and a standard ED triage pattern. They must be corrected or expanded after watching the assigned walk-through video or completing shadowing.

## Door-to-Disposition Workflow

### 1. Patient Arrival

Possible arrival routes:

- walk-in
- ambulance
- referral or transfer
- arrival with family or caregiver

Information entering the system:

- presenting complaint
- visible distress
- arrival method
- referral or ambulance information

Decision point:

- Does the patient show an immediate airway, breathing, circulation, severe bleeding, seizure, or altered-consciousness emergency?

Potential delay or loss:

- queues at the entrance
- incomplete handover
- patient unable to communicate

### 2. Registration

Typical activities:

- identify the patient
- create or locate the patient record
- record demographics
- confirm contact and administrative details

Information entering the system:

- name and identifier
- age
- sex/gender field used by the local system
- contact details
- prior record, when available

Potential delay or loss:

- duplicate records
- spelling or identity errors
- registration completed after urgent clinical care
- paper and electronic records becoming unsynchronised

### 3. Initial Vital Signs

Typical measurements:

- systolic and diastolic blood pressure
- pulse
- temperature
- respiratory rate
- oxygen saturation, when available
- Glasgow Coma Scale or mental-status observation

Decision point:

- Are any values immediately dangerous or inconsistent with the patient's appearance?

Potential delay or loss:

- missing equipment
- interrupted measurement
- manual transcription
- units entered inconsistently
- implausible values accepted without confirmation

### 4. Triage Nurse Assessment

Typical activities:

- confirm chief complaint
- ask symptom-history questions
- review pain and mental status
- consider medical history, medications, pregnancy, allergies, and risk factors
- combine clinical appearance with vital signs

Decision point:

- What acuity or triage level should be assigned?

Potential delay or loss:

- interruptions
- incomplete history
- language or communication barriers
- disagreement between structured rules and clinical judgement

### 5. Triage Priority and Immediate Routing

Possible routes:

- resuscitation or immediate treatment
- urgent clinical assessment
- standard waiting queue
- fast-track or minor-care pathway
- isolation or special pathway

Decision point:

- Is the patient safe to wait, and where should the patient wait?

Potential delay or loss:

- treatment spaces unavailable
- queue priority not updated when the patient's condition changes
- handoff information not reaching the receiving team

### 6. Waiting and Reassessment

Typical activities:

- monitor queue
- repeat observations when required
- respond to deterioration
- update the triage priority

Decision point:

- Has the patient deteriorated or waited beyond the expected threshold?

Potential delay or loss:

- no automated reminder for reassessment
- crowded waiting area
- limited staff visibility
- changes reported informally but not recorded

### 7. Clinician Assessment

Typical activities:

- physician/advanced practitioner review
- focused examination
- order tests, medication, imaging, or treatment
- review the triage record

Decision point:

- What investigation, treatment, and care pathway are required?

Potential delay or loss:

- incomplete triage record
- test-order and result delays
- repeated history-taking

### 8. Disposition

Possible outcomes:

- discharge
- admission
- observation
- transfer
- specialist referral

Decision point:

- Is the patient medically ready and is the next destination available?

Potential delay or loss:

- inpatient bed unavailable
- transport/porter delay
- discharge instructions incomplete
- handoff between ED and receiving service

## Information Handoffs

Key handoffs to validate during observation:

1. Arrival/ambulance staff to registration or triage nurse
2. Registration to triage
3. Triage nurse to treatment-area nurse/clinician
4. Clinician to diagnostics or specialist team
5. ED team to ward, transfer team, or discharged patient

## Observation Questions

1. Which steps happen differently for ambulance and walk-in patients?
2. When are vital signs first recorded, and when do they reach the EHR?
3. How often is triage interrupted?
4. What information is commonly missing at the nurse assessment?
5. What triggers reassessment in the waiting area?
6. How are deteriorating patients identified?
7. Where do paper notes or verbal messages enter the process?
8. Which handoff causes the most delay or repeated work?
9. When does a nurse override the formal triage rule?
10. What would make an AI alert useful rather than distracting?
